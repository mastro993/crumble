# Settings

Part of the [native architecture](native.md): the Settings page.

## Layout

- A sidebar at the panel's leading edge is titled Settings, in 20 pt semibold, and lists the sections in two groups, each under a 15 pt label in the hint ink, a step lighter than secondary text: App holds General, Calendars and Recording, and AI holds Transcription, Summary, Intelligence, Providers and Local Models. Each section is a 30 pt row, 1 pt from the next, with a 16 pt icon, its name in 15 pt regular, and a 10 pt corner radius, all in the selected row's ink so only the fill marks the section on screen; 16 pt separate a group's label from the title or the group above it, and 4 pt from its first row. It sits on the Agenda list's surface with a hairline on its trailing edge. It resizes like the Agenda list and shares its width (see Shell in [native](native.md)), but never collapses: the sidebar toggle and `Cmd-B` do nothing on this page.
- Clicking a row, or Enter or Space on a focused one, shows that section beside the column, under its title, at most 720 pt wide and centered in the space beside the sidebar; the section scrolls as a whole, title included. Settings opens on General, and the rail's Settings button returns to the section picked last while Crumble runs; an Open Settings link elsewhere opens the section it names. Opening Settings or picking a section reads the models folder again, so a Local Model deleted in Finder drops out of the lists. Picking a section is not recorded for Back.
- A section keeps 40 pt of space above its title, at its sides, and below its last group, and 32 pt between its title and its first group. Its settings sit in groups, 44 pt apart, each a heading 16 pt over a card: the theme's card color, the hairline edge controls share and 12 pt corners. A card holds one setting per row, 14 pt above and below it, its title over a muted description on the left and its control on the right, with a fainter hairline between rows; a field beside a setting is 240 pt wide, and a select as wide as its choice up to 240 pt (see [Selects](events.md#selects)). `settings_page/form` draws these parts for every section, and Local Models uses its headings, description and card for its own list, and its search field: a 36 pt pill with the fill and hairline edge every field has, a search icon, 15 pt text, and a button that clears it.

## Settings

- `crates/store` keeps the choices made in Settings, with the rest of the state Crumble keeps across restarts, in the SQLite database `crumble.sqlite3` in the platform data directory (`~/Library/Application Support/Crumble` on macOS), creating the folder when missing. `crates/db` opens the file and runs pending migrations; `crates/store` then reads its tables into memory; views read from memory, and each change is written in its own transaction on the main thread. If the database cannot be opened, migrated, or read, the error is logged and the app runs on the defaults in an in-memory database for that run, leaving the file alone. A stored value that no longer decodes falls back to its default. If a write fails, the error is logged and the app keeps the new value in memory. `settings.json`, used before the database, is no longer read: preferences saved there reset once.
- The tables are `app_state` (one row: `first_day_of_week`, 0 for Monday, `calendar_view`, the Model choices below, and `suggest_recording`), `providers` (what the user changed for each remote Provider, see [Providers](providers.md)), `remote_audio_consents` (see Transcript), `accounts` (by EventKit identifier: `position` in the user's order, `color` as a palette name or `#rrggbb`, `alias`, `disabled`, `hidden`), and `calendars` (by EventKit identifier: `disabled`). An Account or Calendar row is written the first time the user changes it and kept after its values return to the defaults.
- **General** holds **First day of week**, which can be any weekday and defaults to Monday. It sets the column order of every Calendar view and where each week Period begins.
- **Calendars** lists each Account as a group headed by its color dot and name: an Enabled switch, its Alias field, then its Calendars, each with its macOS color, its name, and a switch. Settings is the only place to Disable or Enable an Account or a Calendar. An Account with an Alias is titled with it, followed by its own name in brackets. Each keystroke in the field saves the Alias. A blank Alias, or one equal to the Account's own name, is removed, so the Calendars section's Rename and the field agree. Aliases are saved in `accounts.alias`. Every Account and Calendar starts enabled; turning off an Account's Enabled switch or a Calendar's switch Disables it. A Disabled Account fades its Calendars and keeps their switches as they were. While access is not granted the section says to connect calendars from the Calendars button in the rail, and with no Accounts it says to add them in System Settings › Internet Accounts. Disabled Accounts and Calendars are saved by EventKit identifier in `accounts.disabled` and `calendars.disabled`, and change nothing in macOS.
- **Recording** holds a "Suggest recording when a meeting starts" switch, on until turned off, saved in `app_state.suggest_recording` (see Suggestions). While it is on and macOS keeps Crumble's notifications off, a line under it says "Notifications are off for Crumble in System Settings." with Open System Settings, which opens the Notifications pane on Crumble. Crumble reads the permission at launch, each time its window becomes active, and after each Suggestion.
- **Transcription**, **Summary**, **Intelligence**, **Providers** and **Local Models** ([ADR 0007](../adr/0007-summaries-and-model-providers.md), [ADR 0008](../adr/0008-remote-transcription-and-audio-consent.md), [ADR 0010](../adr/0010-local-models-through-transcribe-cpp-and-handys-list.md), [ADR 0011](../adr/0011-remote-providers-as-a-registry.md)). Providers sets up each remote Provider's key and address; each job section picks who does the job and its Model. Picking a Provider, a Model or a Language transcribes nothing on its own: only Stop does (see Transcript). A job section's Model group has a **Provider** select, then, while a remote Provider or Local Models are picked in it, a **Model** select. The Provider select offers Apple's Model, Local Models for Transcripts, and each remote Provider set up for that job (see Readiness in [Providers](providers.md)), titled with its name and described by what it gets, "Gets the Recording's audio", "Gets the Transcript's text" or "Gets what you ask", or "On this Mac" for an OpenAI-compatible server at a loopback address; a picked Provider that stops being set up stays picked, as an extra row described "Not set up", and its jobs say why.
  - A remote Provider's **Model** select is a combo box of Crumble's suggestions, then the Models the Provider listed, then the saved Model when neither has it. A Model it does not list, such as a new one or one on a server whose list fails, is typed in its search field: while the text, trimmed, is not empty and no row's name is the text, ignoring case, a last row offers "Use “<text>”", the text trimmed and in the case typed, and clicking it, or Enter while it is highlighted, picks that Model, which the select then lists. It shows the saved Model, else the Provider's default, else "Choose a Model". A row picks the Model it shows, even after the Provider lists its Models while the menu is open. Picking one, listed or typed, saves it in the Provider's `providers` row for that job, even the default, so each Provider keeps its own Model for each job; it forgets the Provider's failures for that job, and a Summary Model also starts the Summary backfill. Showing a section whose picked remote Provider is set up, and has not listed its Models this run, lists them once, in the background, with the saved key, and a listing that passes starts the Summary backfill (see Summary); a failure says "Couldn't list its Models. <why>" under the select until the Provider's key or address changes, or Settings shows a section again, by a link, the rail, Back or Forward, or the sidebar, which lists them again.
  - **Transcription**: its **Provider** select offers "Apple, on this Mac", "Crumble, on this Mac" for Local Models while one is downloaded or they are picked, and each remote Provider set up to transcribe, in Settings' order; Apple's until another is picked. Under it: "Local Models keep the audio on this Mac. Remote Providers receive the Recording's audio.", then a Get more Models link, which opens Local Models, and an Add a Provider link, which opens Providers. Picking one saves its id in `app_state.transcription_provider`, `local` for Local Models. For Local Models the **Model** select lists the downloaded Local Models by name and saves the picked one's slug in `app_state.local_model`; picking Local Models while no downloaded one is picked takes the first downloaded. With none downloaded it says "No Local Model is downloaded."
  - **Language** is a searchable select of the picked Model's languages: for a Local Model, "System (<language>)", "Detect automatically" when the Model can tell the language itself, then its languages by name; for Apple's Model and remote Providers, "System (<language>)" then every locale SpeechAnalyzer transcribes, by name. It is saved in `app_state.transcription_locale`, `NULL` for System and `auto` for Detect automatically. A Local Model reads only a locale's language, so `it_IT` picks Italian. A saved locale the picked Model does not list stays, as an extra row, and its Transcripts fail saying so. Picking one forgets the Transcripts that failed, so they can be tried again.
  - Under them, "12 Recordings have no Transcript." with **Transcribe all**, which queues every finished Recording without a Transcript, not already waiting, to the Transcription Model; a remote one asks once, "Send 12 Recordings to <Provider>?", before any is sent. It is disabled while there is none, or the Model lacks what it needs.
  - **Summary**: its **Provider** select offers "Apple, on this Mac" and each remote Provider set up to make Summaries. Apple's row is disabled while its Model cannot run, and a line under the select says why, such as "Apple isn't available: Turn on Apple Intelligence in System Settings."; the select follows when that changes while Crumble runs, and when Apple's Model becomes able to run its failures are forgotten and the backfill starts. Crumble notices only while Settings shows, whichever section is on screen, and asks macOS at most once a second. Under it: "Remote Providers receive the Transcript's text." and Add a Provider. Until one is picked it shows Apple's when that can run, else "Choose a Provider". Picking one saves its id in `app_state.summary_provider`, forgets the previous one's failures, and, when it runs locally (see Summary), summarizes every Note whose Transcript has speech but no Summary.
  - **Intelligence** picks the Model Crumble's Chat talks to: its **Provider** select offers "Apple, on this Mac" and each remote Provider set up to chat. Apple's row is disabled while its Model cannot run, with the same line as Summary's saying why. It shows "Choose a Provider" until one is picked, and saves its id in `app_state.intelligence_provider`. Under it: "The Model Crumble's Chat talks to. Remote Providers receive what you ask." and Add a Provider. The Chat page does not use it yet.
  - **Providers** lists every remote Provider, those set up under Connected above the rest under Available, with the jobs it does and Set up or Edit, which opens its dialog: its API key and its address. Each Provider's address, and the Model each job section picked for it, are saved in its `providers` row, and its key in the Keychain under the service `io.github.mastro993.crumble.api-keys`, never in the database. [Providers](providers.md) describes the list, the dialog and how a key is checked.
  - **Local Models** lists the Models in Handy's list, under a search field that matches words of the name, a streaming toggle that keeps only Models that can stream, a language combo box with a globe before its choice, "All languages" first, and a sort select: Recommended, Accuracy or Speed, best first, or Size, smallest first; picking the same order again flips it, and the select's icon, bars widening down or narrowing, shows which way it runs. Models that tie keep their order. They scroll with the rows, and only the rows in view are drawn, though each is measured once so the scrollbar spans the whole list; changing any of them shows the section from its top. Downloaded comes first, with Apple's built-in Model at its top ("Built into macOS", no scores, never deleted), then the downloaded Local Models; then Available to download. Both groups follow the sort; Recommended, the default, puts Handy's recommended Models first, in its order, and leaves the rest in the list's. Each group is one card, a row per Model with a hairline between rows. A row has its name, with a small filled star after it when Handy recommends the Model and it is not downloaded ("Recommended" as its tooltip), and "No longer offered" at the top right for one a sync dropped; its description; then Accuracy and Speed from Handy's scores as five dots each and, past a short line, its details: its languages ("English only", "99 languages"), Streaming, and Rough timestamps for a Model without times of its own; with its size and buttons at the bottom right. An Available row has Download, 84 pt wide. Pressed, it turns the row fill's color and fills with the button's color from the left as the download arrives, showing the percentage in place of Download, without changing width; under the pointer it reads Cancel, which stops the download and keeps what arrived for the next Download. A failed download shows why in red under the description, and Download again. A downloaded row has Delete, which asks first and, for the Model in use, picks Apple's, then Use, 84 pt wide. The Model in use shows "In use" with a check in Use's place, as wide, white on solid green in light mode and green on a green tint in dark, and so does Apple's. Use makes it the Transcription Model. Several downloads can run at once and keep going when Settings closes. Under the list: "Model list and scores from Handy · Speed measured on Handy's reference laptop.", linking Handy.
