# Native architecture

Crumble is a GPUI desktop application built with GPUI Kit components. The app shell layout is copied from [SkillBinder](https://github.com/mastro993/SkillBinder).

## Ownership

| Package | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `apps/desktop` | Native lifecycle: window options, menus, key bindings, and quitting after the last window closes |
| `crates/ui` | GPUI root view: shell, sidebar, header, navigation history, pages, user settings, and embedded icon assets |
| `crates/theme` | Colors, typography, and dimensions |
| `crates/eventkit` | Read-only bridge to macOS EventKit: calendar access, Accounts, Calendars, Events, and change notifications as plain Rust data |

## Shell

- The macOS window uses a transparent titlebar. The traffic lights sit centred in the 50 pt header row. Other platforms keep native decorations.
- The sidebar toggle stays pinned beside the traffic lights whether the sidebar is open or closed. `Cmd-B` (`Ctrl-B` elsewhere) also toggles it. Opening and closing slide over 220 ms, and the slide is skipped when the system asks for reduced motion.
- You can resize the sidebar between 192 and 400 pt by dragging its trailing edge, or with the arrow, Home, and End keys on the focused handle. It starts at 232 pt. Width and collapsed state are not persisted.
- The back and forward arrows sit in the sidebar's header row. `Cmd-[` and `Cmd-]` (`Ctrl-[` and `Ctrl-]` elsewhere) do the same. They move through an in-memory route history, and each arrow is disabled when there is nothing to go back or forward to.
- The sidebar lists Home, Calendar, and Chat at the top, the Calendars section below them (see Events), and Settings pinned to the bottom. The sections scroll between the top rows and Settings; a hairline divider shows on either side while rows are scrolled out of view behind it, and the scrollbar fades in only while the pointer is over the sidebar. Clicking a section's title, or pressing Enter or Space on it, folds its rows away with a short slide and a turning chevron, and the folded sections are saved in `settings.json`. The section layout is copied from SkillBinder. Clicking a row, or pressing Enter or Space while it has focus, opens its route and records the previous one for Back. The app starts on Home. Home and Chat show only their title for now.
- The theme follows the window's light or dark appearance.
- The header and the page share 24 pt horizontal padding (`CONTENT_PADDING`), so header controls line up with page content. With the sidebar collapsed, the header's leading edge moves right to clear the sidebar toggle.
- Double-clicking empty header space zooms the window, and dragging it moves the window. Presses on header buttons and tabs stay with the control, so they never zoom or drag the window.

## Calendar

Terms such as Calendar view, Period, and Focused date are defined in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

- The Calendar page shows one Period in a Week, Month, or Year Calendar view. It opens on Month view showing today. The Calendar view and Focused date stay in memory while the app runs and are not persisted.
- On the Calendar route the header holds Today, Previous, and Next buttons, the Period title, and a segmented Week | Month | Year switcher. The title truncates when the header is too narrow.
- `Cmd-T` jumps to today and `Cmd-←` / `Cmd-→` step one Period (`Ctrl` elsewhere). These shortcuts are handled only while the Calendar page is open.
- Week view is an hourly grid from 00:00 to 24:00 that scrolls on its own. It opens scrolled to 08:00 and keeps its scroll position while the app runs. Month view stretches 4–6 week rows to fill the page and mutes days from neighbouring months. Clicking a day opens its week in Week view; clicking an Event still opens its details. Year view shows twelve months as rounded cards in a 4×3 grid. Each card always has six week rows, so the cards in a grid row end level, and the grid stays at most 1200 pt wide. The current month's name is bold. Clicking anywhere on a month card opens that month in Month view. Clickable days and month cards show a pointer cursor.
- Week and Month grids sit in a bordered frame with 12 pt rounded corners (`SURFACE_RADIUS`). Shaded cells in the corners follow the curve, including while the Week hours scroll.
- Today is circled in the accent color. Weekends (Saturday and Sunday) are shaded in every Calendar view. In Year view, adjacent weekend columns share one rounded strip.
- Today is read from the local clock on every render, so a window left open past midnight updates on its next redraw.
- Week and Month views draw the Events of the dates on screen, from every Calendar that is not Disabled and whose Account is neither Disabled nor Hidden; Year view draws none. Each Event takes its Account's color (see Events), so every Calendar of an Account shares it. The Events are read off the main thread again when the Period, the Calendar view, the First day of week, a Disabled or Hidden choice, or EventKit changes. A failed read is logged and the last Events stay.
- In Week view, timed Events are blocks placed by time over their day's column, at least 22 minutes tall so short ones stay clickable. Overlapping Events share the column side by side. Blocks under 45 minutes show only their title. An Event crossing midnight is drawn on each day it touches, and one that ends at midnight stays on the day before. All-day Events sit in a strip between the day names and the hours, two per day before "+N more".
- In Month view, each day lists its all-day Events as tinted bars, then its timed Events by start as a colored dot, the start time, and the title. A day shows three before "+N more".
- Clicking an Event opens a popover with its title, when it happens, its Calendar, its location, and a Join button when it has a meeting link. Crumble never changes Events.

## Events

Crumble reads Events from macOS EventKit and never changes them; see [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-read-only-events-via-eventkit.md). Account, Calendar, and Event are defined in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

- `crates/eventkit` is the only crate that calls Objective-C. Every EventKit call is `unsafe`, so it sets its own lints with `unsafe_code = "deny"` and local allows, while the other crates keep the workspace's `forbid`. On other platforms it reports `Access::Unsupported` and returns nothing.
- Calls go through one shared event store. EventKit exceptions, raised when its connection to the calendar daemon drops, and nils from getters declared non-null become errors instead of crashing the app.
- Accounts are the macOS sources that hold at least one event Calendar, sorted by name. Their provider is guessed: Exchange is Outlook, a CalDAV source titled "iCloud" is Apple, and a CalDAV title containing "gmail" or "google" is Google. Any other CalDAV source is Google when one of its Events within 90 days of today has an external identifier ending in `@google.com`, which catches Google Workspace accounts on their own domain. Anything else is Other.
- EventKit has no email field either, so an Account's email is inferred: the user's own `mailto:` address as organizer or attendee of an Event within 90 days of today, read only for CalDAV and Exchange sources, else the one Calendar named after an address, as Google names an Account's main Calendar. Two Calendars named after addresses are ambiguous, because shared Calendars carry other people's addresses, so neither is used.
- Calendar colors are converted to sRGB. Events are returned one per occurrence, with declined and cancelled ones left out. All-day Events are whole dates; timed Events are in the system time zone. A join link is the first `meet.google.com`, Teams, Zoom, or Webex link found in the Event's URL, then its location, then its notes.
- `CalendarSync`, owned by the root view, holds the access state, the Accounts, and the Calendars. At launch it reads them only if access is already granted, so Crumble never prompts on its own. It reads again whenever EventKit reports a change, off the main thread. A failed read is logged and the last data stays on screen.
- The sidebar's Calendars section sits below Chat. Its rows are the Accounts that are not Disabled, each after a color dot and labelled with its Alias when it has one, else with its email when one is found, else with its System Settings name. The dot starts with a color from SkillBinder's folder palette picked from a hash of the Account's identifier, so it looks random but stays put without being saved. Clicking a row, or pressing Enter or Space while it has focus, Hides or Shows the Account. Hidden Accounts are kept in memory only, so every Account that is not Disabled shows again at the next launch, and Disabling and Enabling a Hidden Account leaves it Hidden. Dragging a row reorders the Accounts without changing whether it is Hidden: the row floats under the pointer while the others slide aside, and the order is saved in `account_order` and also used by Settings. Disabled Accounts keep their place in that order while they are left out of the sidebar. Accounts not in the saved order follow, sorted by name. Right-clicking a row opens a context menu (see Context menus) that Hides or Shows the Account, renames it, has a Color submenu with a dot per color and a check on the current one, and moves it up or down. Below the palette, the Custom row opens a third menu level beside the submenu (see Context menus) that holds GPUI Kit's headless color picker, laid out as GPUI Kit lays out its own palette: a row of shades from dark to light for each of nine hues, then a hex field beside the hovered or current color. Choosing a shade, or entering a hex value and pressing Enter, picks that color, saves it, and closes the menu. A custom color drops any transparency and looks the same in light and dark mode, while each palette color has a lighter tone for dark mode. Custom leads with an empty ring until a custom color is picked, then with its dot and a check. Move Up is disabled on the first row and Move Down on the last. The row keeps its hover fill while its menu is open. Rename turns the row into a field holding its name, all selected: Enter or clicking away saves it, and Escape cancels. The drag and its slide are copied from SkillBinder. A picked color is saved in `account_colors`, marks the Account in Settings, and colors its Events on the Calendar page. A Hidden Account's row is dimmed. As in SkillBinder, no keyboard shortcut opens the menu. Before access is asked the section shows one "Connect calendars" row that opens the macOS prompt. After a refusal it shows "Allow calendar access", which opens Privacy & Security › Calendars. With access and no Accounts it shows a hint that opens Internet Accounts. When every Account is Disabled it shows a hint that opens Settings. Accounts are added and removed in System Settings, so the section has no add or delete action. On platforms without EventKit the section is hidden.
- macOS grants calendar access only to an app bundle whose `Info.plist` has `NSCalendarsFullAccessUsageDescription`. `apps/desktop/Info.plist` holds it, and the macOS Cargo runner (`scripts/macos-runner.sh`) wraps `crumble` in `Crumble.app`, signs it, and launches it with `open`. Ctrl-C quits the app too. Test binaries run directly.

## Context menus

- Crumble draws its own context menu (`native_view/menu`) instead of GPUI Kit's `PopupMenu`, which fixes its row height, separators, and corner radius, and has no opening animation.
- A menu opens with its top-left corner at the pointer and stays at least 8 pt inside the window. It has 12 pt rounded corners (`SURFACE_RADIUS`), 8 pt of padding, a hairline edge drawn as a ring so a submenu covers it, and a soft shadow. Its surface is the popover color in light mode and the muted color in dark mode, a step above the sidebar. The metrics are in `crumble_theme::menu`.
- Rows reuse the sidebar row height and icon metrics: 32 pt tall with a 16 pt icon in the muted color. Labels are 13 pt, the macOS menu size. A checked row shows a check at its trailing edge, and a row with a submenu shows a chevron. Enabled rows show the pointing-hand cursor; disabled rows are dimmed, keep the arrow, and cannot be highlighted. Separators are hairlines inset to the row width.
- The menu fades in while sliding 4 pt down onto its place, using the theme's fast duration and enter curve (120 ms). The motion is skipped when the system asks for reduced motion. Submenus and panels appear without motion, and a menu closes without motion.
- A submenu opens when the pointer moves onto its row, when its row is clicked, or with Right, Enter, or Space, which also highlight its first row. It overlaps the menu's edge by 4 pt with its first row level with the row it opened from, and it flips to the left when the window has no room on the right. When the pointer moves onto another row, including a disabled one, the submenu stays open for 150 ms, so a diagonal move toward one of its lower rows can cross the rows in between. Only one level of submenu is supported, but a submenu row can open a panel of any content beside it as a third level, such as the Custom color picker. A panel opens and closes as a submenu does, with the same 150 ms delay, and Right, Enter, or Space open it and move focus into it. Keys used in the panel, such as a hex field's, stay in it and never close the menu. Escape or Left closes the panel and gives focus back to the menu, which also takes focus back when the panel closes while holding it.
- The menu takes keyboard focus. Up and Down move the highlight, skipping separators and disabled rows and wrapping around. Left closes the submenu while the highlight is in it. Enter or Space chooses the highlighted row. Escape closes the submenu, or the menu when no submenu is open.
- Choosing a row closes the menu and then runs it, so an action that moves focus, such as Rename, keeps it. Otherwise focus returns to where it was before the menu opened, or to the root view when that element is gone, so the window's shortcuts keep working.
- While a menu is open, a backdrop covers the window: a press anywhere outside the menu closes it and does not reach what is underneath, and the content underneath does not scroll or show hover. The menu also closes when Tab moves focus away, when a shortcut such as `Cmd-B` runs while focus is not in a panel, or when the app goes to the background.

## Settings

- Settings are saved as `settings.json` in the platform config directory (`~/Library/Application Support/Crumble` on macOS). A missing, unreadable, or partial file falls back to the defaults. If a save fails, the error is logged and the app keeps the new value in memory.
- **First day of week** can be any weekday and defaults to Monday. It sets the column order of every Calendar view and where each week Period begins.
- **Calendars** lists every Account with an Alias field and a switch, and under it its Calendars, each with a checkbox, its macOS color, and its name. Settings is the only place to Disable or Enable an Account or a Calendar. An Account with an Alias is titled with it, followed by its own name in brackets. Each keystroke in the field saves the Alias. A blank Alias, or one equal to the Account's own name, is removed, so the sidebar's Rename and the field agree. Aliases are saved in `account_aliases`. Every Account and Calendar starts enabled; turning an Account's switch off or unchecking a Calendar Disables it. A Disabled Account greys its Calendars out and keeps their checkboxes as they were. The group is hidden while access is not granted. The Disabled Accounts and Calendars are saved as sets of EventKit identifiers (`disabled_accounts`, `disabled_calendars`) and change nothing in macOS.

## Agent automation

- `cargo run -p crumble --features mcp` installs a [gpui-mcp](https://github.com/themixednuts/gpui-mcp) bridge on the main window under the app ID `crumble`. The `gpui-mcp` server in `.mcp.json` finds it, and agents can then read the semantic tree, click, type, and take window screenshots through GPUI's own event pipeline instead of the system mouse. Builds without the feature carry no bridge.
- The bridge needs GPUI automation hooks, so the workspace patches `gpui-pre` with gpui-mcp's copy for every build. The bridge and the patch are pinned to the same gpui-mcp revision as the installed server; update all three together.
- Only elements with an `.id(...)` appear in the tree. In GPUI Kit 0.7.0 a Select's options are drawn but not published, so pick them with the keyboard.
