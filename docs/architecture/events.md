# Events

Part of the [native architecture](native.md): Events, their context menus, and Cards.

## Events

Crumble reads Events from macOS EventKit and never changes them; see [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-read-only-events-via-eventkit.md). Account, Calendar, and Event are defined in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md).

- `crates/eventkit` is the only crate that calls Objective-C. Every EventKit call is `unsafe`, so it sets its own lints with `unsafe_code = "deny"` and local allows, while the other crates keep the workspace's `forbid`. On other platforms it reports `Access::Unsupported` and returns nothing.
- Calls go through one shared event store. EventKit exceptions, raised when its connection to the calendar daemon drops, and nils from getters declared non-null become errors instead of crashing the app.
- Accounts are the macOS sources that hold at least one event Calendar, sorted by name. Their provider is guessed: Exchange is Outlook, a CalDAV source titled "iCloud" is Apple, and a CalDAV title containing "gmail" or "google" is Google. Any other CalDAV source is Google when one of its Events within 90 days of today has an external identifier ending in `@google.com`, which catches Google Workspace accounts on their own domain. Anything else is Other.
- EventKit has no email field either, so an Account's email is inferred: the user's own `mailto:` address as organizer or attendee of an Event within 90 days of today, read only for CalDAV and Exchange sources, else the one Calendar named after an address, as Google names an Account's main Calendar. Two Calendars named after addresses are ambiguous, because shared Calendars carry other people's addresses, so neither is used.
- Calendar colors are converted to sRGB. Events are returned one per occurrence, with declined and cancelled ones left out. All-day Events are whole dates; timed Events are in the system time zone. A join link is the first Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Webex, FaceTime, Whereby, Jitsi Meet, GoTo Meeting, or Amazon Chime link found in the Event's URL, then its location, then its notes, and it carries the service's name. The description is the notes as plain text: HTML, as Google sends a description edited on the web, becomes text, with a link whose text is not its address kept as `[text](address)`; the block of meeting details Google or Teams adds between marker lines goes when it holds a meeting link; and every meeting link left is removed, since the Join button stands for it. Participants are the Event's attendees with their names, else their addresses, and answers, the organizer first; EventKit often lists the organizer among the attendees too, so that entry moves first rather than repeating. An Event without attendees has no participants. Each Event's `id` is unique per occurrence within one run but changes when the Event moves. To find an Event again, it also carries `series_uid`, its iCalendar UID with any `/RID=<seconds>` suffix of a detached occurrence removed, `None` until the Event syncs to its server; and, for a repeating or detached occurrence, `occurrence`, its original start, which moving it does not change: a local date for an all-day one, the wall time read as UTC for a floating one with no time zone, else an instant. [ADR 0004](../adr/0004-event-identity-for-notes.md) has the rules.
- EventKit is read only in `crates/sync`. The root view asks its loaders for Events with the dates and the shown Calendar ids, and redraws when they finish reading. `CalendarSync`, owned by the root view, holds the access state, the Accounts, and the Calendars. At launch it reads them only if access is already granted, so Crumble never prompts on its own. It reads again whenever EventKit reports a change, off the main thread. A failed read is logged and the last data stays on screen.
- The sidebar's Calendars section sits below Chat. Its rows are the Accounts that are not Disabled, each after a checkbox in the Account's color and labelled with its Alias when it has one, else with its email when one is found, else with its System Settings name. The checkbox is filled with the color and holds a check while the Account is shown, and is only outlined in it while the Account is Hidden. The color starts as one from SkillBinder's folder palette picked from a hash of the Account's identifier, so it looks random but stays put without being saved. Clicking a row, or pressing Enter or Space while it has focus, Hides or Shows the Account; the row reads as a checkbox to assistive technology. Hidden Accounts are saved in `accounts.hidden`, and Disabling and Enabling a Hidden Account leaves it Hidden. Dragging a row reorders the Accounts without changing whether it is Hidden: the row floats under the pointer while the others slide aside, and the order is saved in `accounts.position` and also used by Settings. Disabled Accounts keep their place in that order while they are left out of the sidebar. Accounts not in the saved order follow, sorted by name. Right-clicking a row opens a context menu (see Context menus) that renames the Account, has a Color submenu with a dot per color and a check on the current one, and moves it up or down. Below the palette, the Custom row opens a third menu level beside the submenu (see Context menus) that holds GPUI Kit's headless color picker, laid out as GPUI Kit lays out its own palette: a row of shades from dark to light for each of nine hues, then a hex field beside the hovered or current color. Choosing a shade, or entering a hex value and pressing Enter, picks that color, saves it, and closes the menu. A custom color drops any transparency and looks the same in light and dark mode, while each palette color has a lighter tone for dark mode. Custom leads with an empty ring until a custom color is picked, then with its dot and a check. Move Up is disabled on the first row and Move Down on the last. The row keeps its hover fill while its menu is open. Rename turns the row into a field after its checkbox, holding its name, all selected: Enter or clicking away saves it, and Escape cancels. The drag and its slide are copied from SkillBinder. A picked color is saved in `accounts.color`, marks the Account in Settings, and colors its checkbox and its Events on the Calendar page. As in SkillBinder, no keyboard shortcut opens the menu. Before access is asked the section shows one "Connect calendars" row that opens the macOS prompt. After a refusal it shows "Allow calendar access", which opens Privacy & Security › Calendars. With access and no Accounts it shows a hint that opens Internet Accounts. When every Account is Disabled it shows a hint that opens Settings. Accounts are added and removed in System Settings, so the section has no add or delete action. On platforms without EventKit the section is hidden.
- macOS grants calendar access only to an app bundle whose `Info.plist` has `NSCalendarsFullAccessUsageDescription`. `apps/desktop/Info.plist` holds it, and the macOS Cargo runner (`scripts/macos-runner.sh`) wraps `crumble` in `Crumble.app`, signs it, and launches it with `open`. Ctrl-C quits the app too. Test binaries run directly.

## Context menus

- Crumble draws its own context menu (`native_view/menu`) instead of GPUI Kit's `PopupMenu`, which fixes its row height, separators, and corner radius, and has no opening animation.
- A menu opens with its top-left corner at the pointer, or 4 pt below the leading edge of the button that opens it, as the Note actions button does, and stays at least 8 pt inside the window. It has 12 pt rounded corners (`SURFACE_RADIUS`), 8 pt of padding, a hairline edge drawn as a ring so a submenu covers it, and a soft shadow. Its surface is the popover color in light mode and the muted color in dark mode, a step above the sidebar. The metrics are in `crumble_theme::menu`.
- Rows reuse the sidebar row height and icon metrics: 32 pt tall with a 16 pt icon in the muted color. Labels are 13 pt, the macOS menu size. A checked row shows a check at its trailing edge, and a row with a submenu shows a chevron. Enabled rows show the pointing-hand cursor; disabled rows are dimmed, keep the arrow, and cannot be highlighted. Separators are hairlines inset to the row width.
- The menu fades in while sliding 4 pt down onto its place, using the theme's fast duration and enter curve (120 ms). The motion is skipped when the system asks for reduced motion. Submenus and panels appear without motion, and a menu closes without motion.
- A submenu opens when the pointer moves onto its row, when its row is clicked, or with Right, Enter, or Space, which also highlight its first row. It overlaps the menu's edge by 4 pt with its first row level with the row it opened from, and it flips to the left when the window has no room on the right. When the pointer moves onto another row, including a disabled one, the submenu stays open for 150 ms, so a diagonal move toward one of its lower rows can cross the rows in between. Only one level of submenu is supported, but a submenu row can open a panel of any content beside it as a third level, such as the Custom color picker. A panel opens and closes as a submenu does, with the same 150 ms delay, and Right, Enter, or Space open it and move focus into it. Keys used in the panel, such as a hex field's, stay in it and never close the menu. Escape or Left closes the panel and gives focus back to the menu, which also takes focus back when the panel closes while holding it.
- The menu takes keyboard focus. Up and Down move the highlight, skipping separators and disabled rows and wrapping around. Left closes the submenu while the highlight is in it. Enter or Space chooses the highlighted row. Escape closes the submenu, or the menu when no submenu is open.
- Choosing a row closes the menu and then runs it, so an action that moves focus, such as Rename, keeps it. Otherwise focus returns to where it was before the menu opened, or to the root view when that element is gone, so the window's shortcuts keep working.
- While a menu is open, a backdrop covers the window: a press anywhere outside the menu closes it and does not reach what is underneath, and the content underneath does not scroll or show hover. The menu also closes when Tab moves focus away, when a shortcut such as `Cmd-B` runs while focus is not in a panel, or when the app goes to the background.

## Cards

- A card floats over the window with content such as an Event's details, opened by resting the pointer on an element or by clicking a button. Every card comes from `native_view/card`, so they share one look: the context menu's surface, corners, edge, shadow, and opening motion (see Context menus), with 16 pt of padding, twice a menu's, and the window's line height whatever the element it opens from uses.
